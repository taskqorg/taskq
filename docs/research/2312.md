# #2312 Throughput limit: parallel workers and merge queue

## Question

Throughput on the taskq board is 30–50 closes a day. Which stage limits it: ready-to-spawn admission, host concurrency, or the merge queue (ruleset: build 2, merge 1, ALLGREEN)? Change only the proven bottleneck.

## Method

Window: 2026-10-27 00:00Z to 2026-11-03 (7 days; 2026-10-27 is a partial day).

- Board: every trusted `taskq:` entry of the 310 taskq issues updated in the window (GraphQL, first 100 comments per issue). Stage times come from entry pairs: ready entry (`start`, `requeue`, `gone`, `usage-limit`, `publication-failed`) to `spawn worker`; `spawn` to `result`/`ask`/`requeue`/`later`; `result` to `spawn reviewer`; `publish` to `close`. A `start` of a task whose body has `Depends on` is counted apart: that wait is the dependency, not admission.
- Merge queue: all 304 `tests` workflow runs with event `merge_group` (Actions API), PR timelines with `AddedToMergeQueueEvent`, `RemovedFromMergeQueueEvent` and `mergedAt` (290 PRs), failed job logs.
- CoreQ telemetry: Mac `universal.sqlite3`, read-only. Worker `heartbeat`, `spawn` and `slot-*` events exist only from 2026-11-03 06:35Z (the release that records them), so host-wide occupancy across all projects is measured over that 10 h only. Windows and pds18 telemetry were not read (outside the worker's folders): host-wide occupancy there is unknown; the board admission wait below covers their effect on taskq tasks.
- Capacity: Mac `host.json` `admission.max` 10, runtime limits 10 each, load gate CPU 90 % / memory 5 %, 30 s launch spacing.

### A2 verdict

The dominant limit is task intake. Closes track starts day by day within about 10 %, and the system idles while it waits for work: the taskq ready queue is empty 70 % of the time, no-dependency admission takes under a minute at the median, and taskq workers are absent 65–67 % of the time on each primary host.

Rejected, with numbers:

- **Merge queue grouping.** The queue is busy 17 % of wall time. A merge takes one CI run (median 6.7 min from first enqueue). At 6.4 min per serial merge the ruleset admits about 220 merges a day; the board used 40–54. Raising `max_entries_to_merge` or `max_entries_to_build` removes no wait that limits closes; it would only make one red entry fail a group.
- **Host concurrency.** No-dependency ready-to-spawn is 0.8 min median, 3.4 min p90 on all hosts together. Mac host-wide occupancy is 4.3 of 10 on average (max 7). Raising limits or `admission.max` changes nothing while the ready queue is empty. The 30 s launch spacing is the most frequent Mac refusal, but it adds at most seconds per launch and is inside the 0.8 min median.
- **Review and publication.** Reviewer spawn 0.7 min, review 2.6 min, publish to close 7.2 min at the median: each below the 13.4 min worker attempt, and none queues behind a full slot.

Secondary costs that lengthen lead time, not daily throughput: dependency chains (92 h of `Depends on` waits), asks (187 answers, median 2.6 min, 41 h total), and flaky queue runs (21 of 24 failures not real, 24 extra worker attempts).

## Options

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| 1. No queue or capacity change; file the measured queue waste as defects | Follows A2: no change without evidence; each defect gets its own root cause | Closes per day stay intake-bound |
| 2. Fix the recurring flaky tests and the macOS timeout inside this task | Cuts the 7.9 % failure rate and about 15 extra attempts a week | Not the bottleneck A3 targets; root causes unknown (the macOS flake is a sandboxed telemetry read that reports `Telemetry: unknown`), so this task would grow into unrelated debugging |
| 3. Raise merge-queue `max_entries_to_merge` | Simple ruleset change | Rejected by the 17 % busy figure; larger groups fail together |
| 4. Raise host limits or `admission.max` | Simple host change | Rejected: ready queue empty 70 %, Mac at 4.3 of 10 |

## Recommendation

Option 1. Keep the merge-queue ruleset (build 2, merge 1) and host limits as they are: A2 found no queue or capacity wait that limits closes, so A3 has no change to make. Throughput rises only with more started tasks, which is an intake decision for the PM, not a TaskQ change. The measured waste in the queue path goes to the two defect tasks below; the A4 re-measure belongs to them (failed-run rate 7.9 %, 24 extra attempts a week as the baseline).
